Key Findings
👥
Social media does not independently harm productivity
Near-zero bivariate r = −0.007 between daily usage and actual productivity score.
🏆
Job satisfaction is the dominant driver
r = +0.874 — far greater impact than any digital behaviour variable.
🌍
Tipping point at ~4h/day usage
Effect is flat below 4h then non-linearly accelerates. TikTok/Instagram show highest usage (3.07–3.12h/day).
⚖️
Sleep loss amplifies social media harm
Optimal 7–8h sleep → productivity 8.0/10. Below 5h → 4.2/10. Synergistic not additive.
📈
At-Risk cluster: hidden burnout
3.75× higher stress than Balanced users, identical output — unsustainable performance.

🏁
Conclusion
Evidence-Based Verdict
"Social media is a conditional amplifier — not an independent cause — of productivity loss."
High usage→ Sleep loss→ Low output
Job satisfaction (r=+0.874) is the primary driver
RF model explains 77.66% of productivity variance
Protect sleep — the primary mediator of harm

⚠️
Limitations
Cross-sectional
Causation cannot be established — reverse causality possible.
Self-report bias
Usage underestimated ~20–30% vs device logs.
Generalisability
No country/occupation detail beyond broad job-type categories.
Model overfitting
RF training R²≈0.97 vs validation R²≈0.78. Further regularisation advised.
Synthetic data
Near-zero correlations suggest limited real-world structure in dataset.

✅
Recommendations
For Individuals
Protect 7–8h sleep — primary mediator of social media harm
Keep usage under 2h/day; monitor DAI = (usage × stress) ÷ sleep
Address stress first — greater gains than usage restriction alone
Platform-agnostic caps — no evidence to ban specific apps
For Employers
Invest in job satisfaction (r=+0.874) — highest ROI lever available
Screen stress + sleep + usage combined, not usage alone
Structured comms windows reduce Notification Pressure Score
For Platform Designers
Design for intentional engagement — usage feedback & break prompts
Default night-mode & Do Not Disturb to opt-out, not opt-in
Detect doomscrolling signatures; surface proactive break interventions
